Five Sharpest Feedbacks

1
UX · Onboarding
The homepage forces login before showing value — killing top-of-funnel conversion
Observed
On visiting subspace.money, the landing screen immediately shows a login modal (Continue with WhatsApp or phone number). There is no preview of deals, no visible savings examples, no social proof — just a login wall. The "Favourite Brands" and "Shared Subscriptions" sections below are placeholder skeletons that only load post-auth. A first-time visitor sees nothing of value before being asked to hand over their phone number.
Problem
This is a classic conversion killer. The value proposition — "up to 80% off Netflix, Prime, Hotstar" — is not shown before the ask. For a consumer fintech app where trust is everything, asking for a phone number with zero demonstrated value increases drop-off dramatically. Users who arrive from word-of-mouth or social ads are not given the "aha moment" before being gated. Subspace is competing with Rocket Money, Wallet, and similar apps globally — all of which show dashboards and live data before sign-up.
Ship Instead
Build a public, unauthenticated homepage that shows: (1) a live ticker of current deals ("Netflix Premium at ₹149/month — 5 slots open"), (2) a sample shared group dashboard with blurred-out member names, and (3) a savings calculator — "Enter your current Netflix spend → See what you'd save on Subspace." Login should be deferred to the moment of transaction intent. This mirrors how Zepto and Blinkit let you browse before forcing sign-in, dramatically lowering friction.
2
Features · Trust & Safety
Shared subscription model has a critical admin accountability gap — users lose money with no recourse
Observed
In the Google Play Store reviews, a user wrote: "Subspace make very bad rule for the user — once if you pay then you lose your money. If the subscriber doesn't allow you to login… subscription doesn't refund the amount." Another review noted: "had problems with the shared subscription as admin never replied." In the shared subscription model, the admin holds total power — they can log the paying member out at any time, and there is no platform-enforced SLA for access or refund.
Problem
The entire shared subscription business model rests on user-to-user trust, but Subspace currently provides no escrow, no admin rating system, no access-guarantee SLA, and no automated refund trigger if access is denied. This is a structural trust deficiency — not a support issue. At scale, even a 5% bad-actor admin rate destroys retention and NPS. For a bootstrapped company with ₹36.5 Cr ARR, this is the single biggest risk to network effect compounding.
Ship Instead
Introduce an "Admin Reliability Score" (visible on group listings) built from: uptime compliance, response time to access complaints, and historical kick-out rate. Implement payment escrow — member funds are held for 48 hours post-payment; released to admin only after access is confirmed via an in-app ping. If access fails within 24 hours, auto-trigger refund. This creates a structurally safe marketplace, not just a reactive support system.
3
GTM · ICP Clarity
The app tries to be three products at once — subscriptions, rentals, and gift cards — but communicates none of them clearly to new users
Observed
The Play Store listing pitches: shared subscriptions (Netflix, Prime), rental delivery in 10 minutes (gadgets), and discounted gift cards (Swiggy, Zomato, Amazon) — all in the same breath. The app navigation has Home, Explore, Wallet, Chat, and Account. On the website, the hero says "Your subscription management platform" but delivery-in-minutes is also front and centre. The product identity is fragmented across three very different use cases with different ICPs and different trust requirements.
Problem
A college student saving on OTT has a completely different mental model than someone renting a power drill for a day. Conflating three products in one app weakens positioning for each — you don't win the "cheapest OTT" user AND the "instant rental" user with the same top-of-funnel message. This dilutes word-of-mouth ("what does Subspace actually do?"), makes referral loops weaker, and makes ad targeting inefficient. Subspace's bootstrapped model requires very high referral-driven acquisition — unclear positioning is the enemy of that.
Ship Instead
Introduce mode-based onboarding: after sign-up, ask "What brings you here?" with three clear paths — "Save on subscriptions," "Rent something," "Get gift cards cheaper." Each path lands on a tailored home feed. This doesn't require splitting the app — just contextual entry points and personalised first sessions. Run a 30-day A/B test: users with personalised onboarding vs. current generic home. Hypothesis: D7 retention improves by 15%+ for the personalised cohort.
4
Competitor Analysis · Features
The Negotiate API — Subspace's strongest moat — is invisible to the user and not leveraged as a growth surface
Observed
Subspace's Negotiate API — which automatically negotiates prices with brands on behalf of users — is mentioned in investor/analyst profiles (Tracxn, the assignment brief itself) but is not prominently surfaced anywhere in the user-facing product. There is no "Negotiate my bill" CTA on the home screen, no success story ("We saved 1,200 users ₹450 on their Hotstar bill this month"), and no opt-in flow that makes the negotiation feel like a distinct, exciting feature. Comparably, Rocket Money's bill negotiation is its hero feature — prominently shown, with success rates disclosed.
Problem
A product moat that users don't know about doesn't drive retention or referrals. The Negotiate API is genuinely hard to replicate — it's the one feature that no Indian competitor has matched. But if a user churns before ever discovering it, or never shares it with a friend, the moat doesn't compound. This is a classic product marketing gap: the most defensible feature is buried rather than being the primary growth engine.
Ship Instead
Make "Negotiate" a first-class surface. Add a persistent "Negotiate a Bill" card on the home screen with a live counter ("₹2.3 Cr saved for users this year"). Build a share-worthy savings certificate — after a successful negotiation, generate an auto-shareable card: "Subspace just saved me ₹380 on my Canva subscription 🎉" with a referral code baked in. This turns every negotiation win into a viral acquisition loop — a flywheel that's both defensible and scalable with zero ad spend.
5
UX · Potential Collaborations
No UPI auto-pay integration for subscription renewals — a massive friction point in the payment loop
Observed
When a user wants to renew a shared subscription on Subspace, they must manually initiate payment each cycle via the app. There is no UPI AutoPay (e-mandate) setup, no auto-debit reminder linked to the renewal date, and no "set and forget" option for recurring group contributions. The website's login modal offers "Continue with WhatsApp" — suggesting deep WhatsApp integration — but this is not extended to payment nudges or auto-pay flows.
Problem
Subscription sharing is a recurring behaviour — but it requires recurring payment friction right now. Every renewal is a churn risk. A user who forgets to pay gets kicked from the group; the group admin loses a paying member; Subspace loses a transaction. For a platform with 90%+ AI-powered ops, the payment loop is ironically the most manual part of the experience. India's UPI AutoPay infrastructure (NACH e-mandates, UPI recurring) makes this entirely solvable — and competitors in the recurring billing space (Razorpay Subscriptions, Cashfree) have already standardised it.
Ship Instead
Integrate UPI AutoPay (e-mandate) at group join time: "Set up auto-pay so you never lose access." Partner with Setu or Cashfree for UPI recurring mandate infrastructure (both have developer-friendly APIs). Additionally, build a WhatsApp payment reminder bot — 3 days before renewal, send a WhatsApp message: "Your Netflix group renews in 3 days. Tap to confirm or switch group." This reduces involuntary churn, increases admin retention, and makes the subscription lifecycle fully automated — consistent with Subspace's "90% AI-native ops" brand promise.
